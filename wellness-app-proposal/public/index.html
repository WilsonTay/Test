Section 01
Executive Summary

The Opportunity

Malaysia faces a converging crisis: an aging population is driving exponential healthcare costs, while the B40 segment—80% of the population—lacks the resources for preventive health management. WellnessMyMalaysia proposes an AI-powered mobile platform that transforms how 26 million citizens manage their health through daily habit tracking, intelligent nutrition guidance, and government-backed financial incentives.

This initiative addresses Malaysia's most pressing public health challenge: the absence of a scalable, trackable, and incentive-driven wellness system for the population that needs it most. Current systems cannot track citizen wellness progress, medical records remain fragmented across providers, and there is no mechanism to measure the effectiveness of preventive health programs at a national scale.

WellnessMyMalaysia solves this by creating a single AI-powered platform that scans daily meals, provides nutritional guidance, tracks behavioral changes over 30-day cycles, and builds a national wellness database—all while rewarding citizens for participating. The model is designed for viral growth through a referral system and can scale across ASEAN.

Core Philosophy

We do not tell people to stop eating nasi lemak. We show them how much water to drink alongside it, how much fiber to add, and how to build small habits that compound into lasting health improvements.

This proposal seeks government funding for a 12-month pilot program with quarterly milestone reviews, measurable KPIs, and a clear path to national-scale deployment and ASEAN expansion.

Section 02
Problem Statement & Market Gap
1

The Healthcare Cost Crisis

Malaysia's aging population is creating unsustainable pressure on government healthcare spending. As citizens age, disease burden increases exponentially, yet the vast majority of expenditure remains reactive—treating illness rather than preventing it.

2

The B40 Paradox

The B40 segment (80% of Malaysians) has the highest disease rates yet cannot afford preventive health management. T20 citizens have the discipline and resources to self-manage; B40 citizens are trapped in a cycle of unaffordable reactive care.

3

Zero Tracking Infrastructure

No centralized system exists to track citizen wellness over time. Medical records are scattered across providers. Government has no mechanism to measure wellness initiative effectiveness. The data simply does not exist.

4

Unregulated Wellness Market

Health product sellers make misleading claims. No standardized wellness coaching certification exists. Citizens lack basic knowledge about nutrition, hydration, and the 8 dimensions of wellness. The market needs structure and education.

The Government's Pain Point

Through direct engagement with MOH, we have identified a fundamental frustration: the government genuinely wants to find partners who can execute, not just propose. Many parties have discussed wellness initiatives before, but none have delivered. MOH has expressed clear willingness to support initiatives that demonstrate real capability and measurable results.

Key Insight from MOH Engagement

"A lot of people have talked about this before. But they haven't done it." — The government is actively seeking partners who can move from proposal to execution with demonstrated commitment and capability.

How It Works
📷

1. Scan Your Meal

Open the app, point your camera at your food. AI instantly recognizes Malaysian dishes—nasi lemak, roti canai, char kuey teow—and calculates nutritional content.

🧠

2. Get Smart Suggestions

Instead of saying "don't eat that," the AI suggests: "Add a glass of water" or "Include fiber-rich vegetables." Build habits, not restrictions.

📈

3. Track 30-Day Progress

Complete a 30-day wellness challenge. See your behavioral changes over time—dietary patterns, hydration habits, nutritional improvements.

🎉

4. Earn RM30 Reward

Complete the program, receive RM30 deposited to your account. Refer friends for another RM30 each. Government-funded incentives drive adoption.

Critical Design Decision: Real-Time Only

Photos must be taken in real-time using the camera only—no gallery uploads permitted. Timestamps and metadata verify authenticity. This ensures data integrity and prevents gaming of the reward system.

Section 16 • Strategic Advisory
McKinsey Perspective: What's Next

The following strategic recommendations are structured as an independent advisory assessment, evaluating the initiative's strengths, gaps, and the critical path to maximizing both impact and commercial success.

1. Secure a "Lighthouse" Win Before Scaling

The single most important next step is demonstrating a small, irrefutable proof of concept before seeking large-scale funding. Government decision-makers are, by your own account, tired of proposals without execution. A 200-person pilot in a single B40 community—run for 30 days with real data—will be more persuasive than any proposal document.

  • Immediate Action: Identify one PPR (Program Perumahan Rakyat) community in KL as pilot site
  • Build an MVP app (food scanning + 30-day tracking only—no medical reports, no referrals yet)
  • Run a 30-day pilot with 200 participants, capturing before/after behavioral data
  • Produce a 5-page data report showing measurable behavioral change
  • Present this report—not the proposal—to MOH at your next meeting
  • Timeline: 4-6 weeks from today

2. Narrow the MVP Ruthlessly—Do Less, Better

The current proposal contains at least 8 major feature categories. This is a common trap for ambitious founders. Every feature you add before launch is a week you delay proving the core hypothesis. The core hypothesis is simple: "Can an AI app change the eating habits of B40 Malaysians in 30 days?"

  • MVP features (launch with ONLY these): AI food scanner, 30-day habit tracker, RM30 reward
  • Phase 2 features (add after proving core): Referral system, basic wellness coaching
  • Phase 3 features (add after 10K users): Medical report scanning, government dashboard
  • Phase 4 features (add after government endorsement): Wellness coach certification, corporate plans, ASEAN expansion
  • This sequencing reduces initial development cost by ~60% and accelerates time-to-market by 8-12 weeks

3. Reframe the Government Pitch: ROI, Not CSR

The current narrative leans toward social good and CSR. While admirable, government budget holders respond to economic ROI and cost avoidance. Reframe the entire pitch around one number: "For every RM1 you invest in WellnessMyMalaysia, we will save RM3-5 in downstream healthcare costs."

  • Lead with economics, not empathy. Open every presentation with the cost-savings model
  • Benchmark against Singapore's HealthHub and Australia's MyHealthRecord for credibility
  • Calculate the actual cost of a single diabetes hospitalization (est. RM15K-30K) vs. RM30 prevention incentive
  • Position the database as a national asset worth RM100M+ within 5 years
  • Frame it as: "You are not funding an app. You are building a national wellness infrastructure."

4. De-Risk Through a Dual-Grant Strategy

Do not put all funding eggs in one basket. The conversation revealed two grant pathways—MOH internal grant and RE-INVEST open grant. Pursue both simultaneously, with different scopes.

  • MOH Internal Grant: Fund the app development + 30-day pilot (tech-focused proposal)
  • RE-INVEST Grant: Fund the Wellness Carnival + physical screening events (event-focused proposal)
  • Apply for MDEC Digital Content Grant for the AI/ML model development component
  • Explore MTDC (Malaysian Technology Development Corporation) for technology commercialization funding
  • Consider Khazanah's Dana Impak or Yayasan Hasanah for social impact component
  • Each grant application should be self-contained—success on any single grant enables Phase 1

5. Build the Data Moat Early—It's Your Real Asset

The app is the vehicle. The data is the asset. From Day 1, every design decision should maximize the quality, depth, and exclusivity of your wellness dataset. This dataset becomes the competitive moat that makes WellnessMyMalaysia irreplaceable.

  • Design data schemas for research-grade output from the start (anonymized, structured, queryable)
  • Partner with one university (UM, UKM, or USM public health faculty) to validate data methodology
  • Publish one peer-reviewed paper from pilot data—this transforms the project from "app" to "research platform"
  • Negotiate data licensing terms with MOH upfront: government gets access, you retain commercial rights
  • Target insurance companies (AIA, Prudential, Great Eastern) as Year 2 data licensing customers

6. Solve the "Champion Problem" Inside MOH

Government initiatives live or die by internal champions. You need one senior MOH officer who personally owns this project's success. Without this, the initiative will stall when personnel rotate or priorities shift.

  • Identify your primary MOH contact's supervisor—brief them directly on the initiative
  • Create a 2-page "Minister-Ready" brief that your MOH champion can escalate internally
  • Offer to present at MOH's internal innovation forum or town hall
  • Make your MOH champion look good—position them as the visionary who brought AI wellness to Malaysia
  • Establish a formal MOU (even non-binding) to create institutional commitment

7. Professionalize the Governance Structure Now

The current structure—operating through an ROS club with mixed personal and organizational interests—creates governance risk that will concern government auditors. This needs to be addressed before large funds flow.

  • Establish a dedicated SPV (Special Purpose Vehicle) company for this project
  • Clearly separate ROS activities, USANA activities, and WellnessMyMalaysia operations
  • Appoint an independent advisory board (include one former government officer)
  • Implement proper financial controls—separate bank accounts, quarterly audits
  • Draft a shareholders' agreement covering IP ownership, profit-sharing, and exit terms
  • This professionalization is not bureaucracy—it's what separates fundable projects from unfundable ones

8. Leverage E100 as a Live R&D Lab

The E100 pitching competition is an underutilized asset. Instead of treating it as a separate initiative, integrate it as the talent pipeline and innovation lab for WellnessMyMalaysia.

  • Offer E100 teams the opportunity to build features for WellnessMyMalaysia as their pitch project
  • Best teams get hired/contracted to work on the platform post-competition
  • Wilson's AI engineering support becomes the prize—not just RM3,000 cash
  • This generates free R&D, identifies talent, and creates media-worthy stories
  • Position E100 as "Malaysia's first AI wellness hackathon" for additional media coverage

9. Build Strategic Alliances Before You Need Them

The current partnership structure is founder-heavy. To scale beyond pilot, you need institutional partnerships that survive personnel changes and create compounding advantages.

  • Healthcare: Partner with one private hospital chain (KPJ, IHH) for medical report integration
  • Insurance: LOI with one insurance company (Hong Leong, AIA) for wellness data pilot
  • Telco: Partner with one telco (Maxis, Celcom) for B40 data subsidies and distribution
  • Banking: Partner with one digital bank (Touch 'n Go, GXBank) for RM30 reward disbursement
  • Media: Secure one media partnership (Astro, Media Prima) for the Wellness Carnival
  • Each partnership letter strengthens the MOH proposal immeasurably

10. Create a 90-Day Sprint Plan—Not a 12-Month Roadmap

The 12-month roadmap is necessary for the proposal but operationally irrelevant. What matters is the next 90 days. Create a hyper-specific 90-day sprint with weekly milestones.

  • Week 1-2: Finalize MVP scope. Wilson builds food scanner prototype. Draft MOH internal grant application.
  • Week 3-4: MVP food scanner working on 50 Malaysian dishes. Identify pilot community. Submit MOH grant.
  • Week 5-6: Beta test with 50 internal users. Fix critical bugs. Draft RE-INVEST grant application.
  • Week 7-8: Launch 200-person pilot in one PPR community. Begin collecting data.
  • Week 9-10: Mid-pilot check-in. Adjust app based on user feedback. Prepare data visualizations.
  • Week 11-12: Complete pilot. Produce impact report. Present results to MOH. Request formal endorsement.
  • Week 13 (Day 91): Decision point—scale or pivot based on pilot data.

Final Advisory Note
The Difference Between a Good Idea and a Funded Initiative

This initiative has genuine potential—it addresses a real problem, targets the right population, and leverages modern AI to do what was previously impossible at scale. However, the gap between "good idea" and "funded, operating initiative" is not bridged by better proposals. It is bridged by demonstrated execution. Build the MVP. Run the pilot. Collect the data.
